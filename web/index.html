SwarmRescue

Decentralized ant-pheromone multi-robot exploration for disaster-zone search and rescue · OptiForge 2026, Track 05

The problem

After an earthquake, a swarm of rescue robots enters a collapsed building (a grid of free floor and debris). The robots must search every reachable cell and find trapped survivors quickly, without collisions and within their battery budget. There is no central controller. Each robot decides its own move from local sensing, a digital pheromone trail of cells already searched, and trails shared over a short-range radio link. When nearby cells are all searched, it takes a BFS route to the nearest unsearched cell. Survivors emit acoustic pings that nearby robots home in on. An aftershock can drop new debris, knock out a robot and cut the radio. Because there is no single point of failure, the swarm keeps searching.

This is a JavaScript port of the Python simulator: the same pheromone score, BFS reroute, deadlock breaker, idle mode, acoustic pings and collision-free sequential moves. Its random numbers differ, so the maps are not the same as the Python seeds.

On 10 held-out maps that PSO never saw, Round 2 fitness rose from 116.50 (Attempt 1, no pings) to 120.26 with acoustic pings, and to 121.03 with pings plus PSO-tuned pheromone evaporation. Survivors found went from 94% to 100%, and the average time to the first survivor fell from 13.9 to 8.5 ticks. Collisions were zero in every run.
